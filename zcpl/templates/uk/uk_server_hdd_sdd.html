SSD vs HDD: Key Differences Between Solid State Drives and Hard Disk Drives

Choosing between an HDD and an SSD comes down to your workload, budget and capacity requirements. Both have a clear role in enterprise environments. Understanding the differences helps you get the right drive in the right slot, rather than overspending on performance you do not need or under-specifying where it counts.

Specification HDD SSD
Best Use High capacity storage, archive, backup High IOPS, low latency, boot drives
Speed 80 to 160 MB/s typical 200 MB/s to 7,000 MB/s (NVMe)
Capacity Up to 20 TB per drive Up to 30.72 TB (NVMe)
Durability Moving parts, more vulnerable to shock No moving parts, more resilient
Power Higher draw Lower draw
Noise Audible during operation Silent
Cost per GB Lower, best for bulk storage Higher, justified by performance
Form Factors 2.5″, 3.5 inch 2.5″, 3.5″, M.2, U.2, PCIe
Interfaces SATA, SAS SATA, SAS, M.2, PCIe NVMe
NVMe Not applicable PCIe 3.0 / 4.0 / 5.0, lowest latency

SAS, SATA or NVMe: Which Interface Do You Need?

Use the guide below to match the interface to your workload and server. Not sure? Call us and we will confirm compatibility before you order.

Specification SAS Serial Attached SCSI SATA Serial ATA NVMe Non-Volatile Memory Express
Best For Storage arrays, SANs, high-availability servers where dual-path redundancy is required Boot volumes, secondary storage, cost-optimised builds and direct-attached storage Databases, OLTP, virtualisation hosts, AI inference, real-time analytics
Transfer Speed Up to 12Gb/s per port; dual-port provides up to 24Gb/s aggregate Up to 6Gb/s (SATA III) Up to 7,000 MB/s sequential; sub-100 µs latency on random I/O
Drive Types HDDs (7.2K, 10K, 15K rpm) and SAS SSDs HDDs and SSDs SSDs only (U.2, M.2, EDSFF form factors)
Dual-Port Yes, multipath I/O eliminates single points of failure Single-port only, not suitable for dual-path configurations Sub-millisecond, up to 10× lower than SAS SSD on random workloads
Compatible Servers Dell PowerEdge, HPE ProLiant, Lenovo ThinkSystem, IBM Virtually all servers and workstations with a SATA backplane or controller Dell PowerEdge (14G onwards), HPE ProLiant Gen10 onwards, Lenovo ThinkSystem
Best choice for high-availability servers and storage arrays where redundant data paths are required. Ideal for OS boot volumes, secondary storage and cost-sensitive builds where dual-path redundancy is not required. Choose NVMe when your workload is I/O-bound. Databases, virtualisation and AI inference benefit most.

Frequently Asked Questions About HDD & Solid State Drives

Answers to the questions we receive most frequently from IT procurement leads and infrastructure managers. Contact us if your query is not addressed below.

1. Which storage type is faster, HDD or SSD?
- SSDs deliver much faster data transfer rates and quicker access times than HDDs.
2. What is the difference between SAS, SATA and NVMe enterprise drives?
- SAS offers dual-port redundancy for storage arrays. SATA is cost-effective for boot and secondary storage. NVMe delivers the lowest latency via PCIe, suited to databases and virtualisation.
3. Are refurbished enterprise drives safe to use in a production server?
- Yes. Every refurbished drive is SMART-tested, stress-cycled and certified before dispatch. Only drives that pass our full test regime are shipped.
4. Do you supply server-specific or OEM-branded hard drives?
- Yes. We supply OEM-branded drives with the correct firmware for Dell, HPE, Lenovo and IBM servers, preventing lock-out errors common with generic drives.
5. Can you identify the correct drive for my server?
- Yes. Share your server make, model and cage type and we will confirm the compatible specification before quoting.
6. What is the minimum order quantity?
- There is no minimum order. We supply single drives for emergency replacements as readily as bulk orders.
