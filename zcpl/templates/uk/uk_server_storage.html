FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Server Storage

1. What is server storage and why does it matter?
Server storage refers to the dedicated hardware used to store, manage and retrieve data within a business or data centre environment. Unlike consumer storage, enterprise server storage is built for continuous operation, high availability and shared access across multiple servers and users.
2. What is the difference between a NAS, SAN, and JBOD?
A NAS (Network-Attached Storage) provides file-level access over standard network protocols such as NFS and SMB. A SAN (Storage Area Network) provides block-level storage over dedicated fabric (Fibre Channel or iSCSI) and is typically used for databases and virtualisation shared datastores. A JBOD (Just a Bunch of Disks) is a raw drive enclosure with no onboard intelligence; it expands capacity by connecting to a separate storage controller or head node.
3. Is refurbished server storage reliable for enterprise use?
Yes. Every unit is fully tested, firmware-updated and certified before dispatch. All refurbished storage ships with warranty cover, giving you the same assurance as new at a significantly lower cost.
4. Can you supply specific Dell or HPE storage models?
Yes. We stock Dell PowerVault and HPE MSA platforms, new and refurbished. If you have a specific model or part number in mind, contact us and we will confirm availability and lead time.
5. Do you offer an all-flash array for AI or database workloads?
Yes. We supply NVMe and SAS SSD all-flash arrays suited for SQL, Oracle, SAP HANA and VDI workloads. Contact us to discuss your IOPS and latency requirements.
6. Can I get ongoing support after purchasing storage hardware?
Yes. All purchases include free 24/7 remote technical support.
7. Which storage is best for enterprise servers?
It depends on the workload. SAN storage is best for virtualisation and databases requiring shared block access. NAS suits file sharing and backup. All-Flash Arrays deliver the highest performance for latency-sensitive applications. Most enterprise environments use a combination of these, sized to the specific workload.
